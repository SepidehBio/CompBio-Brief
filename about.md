    <h2>What this is</h2>

    <p>
      CompBio Lab is a personal research notebook. It gathers notes,
      tool references and short essays produced while working on
      computational biology, bioinformatics and biomedical data analysis.
      It is not a blog in the usual sense; it is closer to a public
      laboratory logbook.
    </p>

    <p>
      The writing here is deliberately plain. The aim is not to impress
      with jargon, but to document what was tried, what worked, and what
      did not. In computational work, the reasoning behind a choice is
      often more valuable than the choice itself.
    </p>

    <h2>Why it exists</h2>

    <p>
      Most analytical work in biology disappears into private notebooks,
      internal wikis or chat threads. When a pipeline needs to be
      re-run six months later — or when a colleague tries to reproduce
      a result — the reasoning is often gone.
    </p>

    <p>
      This site exists to keep that reasoning visible. Each note is meant
      to be readable by someone who was not present when the work was
      done: a future collaborator, a reviewer, or simply a more
      experienced version of the author.
    </p>

    <h2>How it is built</h2>

    <p>
      The site is built from scratch: static HTML pages generated by
      Jekyll, styled with hand-written CSS, and brought to life by a
      small amount of plain JavaScript. There is no frontend framework,
      no CSS library and no tracking script.
    </p>

    <p>
      It is hosted on GitHub Pages and available in three languages:
      English, Danish and Persian. The language is detected
      automatically from the browser and can be changed at any time.
      The site also supports a light and dark theme, chosen by the
      reader and remembered between visits.
    </p>

    <h2>Scope</h2>

    <p>
      Topics covered include sequence analysis, single-cell methods,
      variant interpretation, spatial transcriptomics and the software
      that supports them. If a topic is outside the author's direct
      experience, it does not appear here.
    </p>
